Internal Management Platform — Technical Blueprint v0.2 · Scope revision
Revision 02 · Architecture, schema, roadmap

An internal operations platform for a 92-unit portfolio

The app is no longer a two-sided product. It is a tool used by two kinds of staff — the landlord, who owns the money and the portfolio, and the caretaker, who owns what happens on site. Tenants stay where they already are: WhatsApp, the front gate, and email.

What changed
The tenant portal leaves v1. A restricted Caretaker role enters. Vacancy, electricity tokens and true tenant offboarding become first-class modules. Everything else in v0.1 holds.
What it buys you
Roughly five weeks off the build, no tenant-adoption problem to solve, and a caretaker who can log a repair with photos from a phone in the corridor — which is where repairs are actually reported.
One warning before the detail: hard-deleting a tenant destroys financial history. §4 gives you a safe version of the same button.

The spine is unchanged — Next.js, Postgres, Prisma. Four things move, all because the primary user is now a caretaker holding a phone in a stairwell with one bar of signal.

Unchanged from v0.1 unless marked. Every table keeps id, org_id, created_at, updated_at; money is integer cents in ZAR.

This single column is what makes hard deletion survivable. Do not skip it.

4 · Tenant deletion, done safely

You asked for a button that completely removes a tenant and frees the unit. That is the right operational instinct — stale tenants clutter every list. But a naive DELETE cascades into invoices, payments and ledger rows, and takes last year's income statement with it. Here is the same button, built so it cannot hurt you.

Default — "Offboard & anonymise"
The tenant row is deleted. The lease, invoices, payments and ledger stay, carrying only tenant_display_ref. Photos and messages are stripped of contact detail. The unit flips to Vacant. Every list in the app is clean; the books still balance.
On request — "Erase completely"
Everything above, plus the financial rows themselves. Behind a typed-confirmation dialog, restricted to the landlord, blocked while a balance or deposit is outstanding, and only after the archive PDF has been generated. For a data-deletion request, not for routine move-outs.
The offboarding transaction — one atomic step
01
Landlord opens the tenant, chooses Offboard, sets move-out date and reason. The app shows the exit checklist: final balance, deposit held, open repairs, move-out inspection.
02
Blockers are named, not hidden: an outstanding balance, an unreturned deposit or an open ticket each show as a line the landlord must resolve or explicitly override.
03
Archive PDF generated — the full tenant record, lease, ledger and inspection photos — stored and emailed to the landlord. This is the thing you produce if the tenant disputes a deposit two years later.
04
In one database transaction: write tenant_offboardings with the JSON snapshot → close the lease → delete (or anonymise) the tenant row → set unit.status = 'vacant', vacant_since = move-out date → write unit_status_history → write audit_log. All of it or none of it.
05
The unit appears on the vacancy dashboard the same second, with a days-vacant counter starting from the move-out date, and drops off the caretaker's tenant list.

5 · Core flows, revised

A · Caretaker repair — WhatsApp or doorstep to resolved
01
Tenant reports off-app. A WhatsApp message or a knock on the caretaker's door. Nothing is asked of the tenant — that is the point of this revision.
02
Caretaker logs it in under 40 seconds. Home screen is a single Log a repair button. Pick the unit (recent units first, search by number or tenant name), pick a category from six tiles, type or paste the description, shoot photos with the camera, set urgency. Reported-via defaults to WhatsApp. The tenant's name auto-fills from the lease and the ticket number is shown immediately, ready to send back.
03
Offline is normal. No signal in the stairwell: the ticket and its photos sit in the outbox and sync on reconnect. The caretaker's screen says "1 waiting to upload", never a failure.
04
Landlord is notified, not consulted. Status logged. Emergencies push and SMS immediately; routine work batches into one morning digest. The caretaker can start on anything within their own mandate without waiting.
05
Progress in three taps. The caretaker moves the ticket logged → in_progress → resolved, adding a photo and a one-line note at each step. Each tap writes a maintenance_updates row. Cost fields are not shown to them at all.
06
Landlord closes the money. Reviews before/after photos, records vendor cost and receipt — which posts an expenses row against the property — and closes the ticket. Tenant damage becomes a recharge line on the next invoice in one action.
07
Tenant is told by the caretaker. Optionally, a status-update email if the tenant has an address on file. The photo trail is the record if anyone later says it was never fixed.
B · Electricity capture — slips to a per-unit trend
01
Capture. Landlord buys prepaid electricity and opens Electricity → Add purchase. Two entry modes: a single form (unit, date, rand amount, kWh, token number, receipt photo), or batch mode — a spreadsheet-style grid where twenty units are captured in one sitting, which is how this actually gets done. CSV import from the supplier portal as the fast path.
02
Validate on entry. Duplicate token numbers are rejected. An effective rate more than 20% off that meter's own history raises a soft warning — the cheapest possible detection of a mistyped kWh figure or a broken meter.
03
Recharge, if applicable. Flag recharge to tenant and the purchase writes a line onto that lease's next invoice at cost or at a configured markup, linked back to the purchase row. Unflagged purchases stay a property expense in the P&L.
04
Read the trend. Per unit: purchases, kWh and rand by month, effective rate, and a 12-month sparkline. Per property: total spend, cost per unit, and outliers ranked — a unit consuming double its neighbours is either a geyser fault or an unauthorised occupant, and both are worth a visit.
05
Report. Electricity becomes its own line in the income statement and its own tax category, with the receipt images attached for the accountant.
C · Invoicing — unchanged engine, email-only delivery

The T−7 generate → T−5 issue → T−2 remind → T+0 collect → T+grace late fee → escalate cycle from v0.1 stands exactly as written, with two edits: there is no "Pay now" link and no portal balance, so the emailed PDF must carry the banking details and the outstanding balance prominently; and every payment is recorded by hand by the landlord, oldest-invoice-first, with an emailed receipt as confirmation.

6 · Revised roadmap

Reordered around the new priorities. The caretaker app moves early — it is the smallest piece of software with the largest daily payoff, and it earns staff trust before you touch the money.

P0 · 1–2 d
Foundations
Repo, Prisma, Postgres, CI, Sentry, staging. Seed the real portfolio shape: 3 properties, 92 units, one caretaker account.
P1 · 1–2 wk
Auth, roles, RLS, two shells
Staff login, role-based routing, caretaker_assignments, RLS policies with a permission test suite, and the two UI shells — dense desk, thumb-first mobile. Write the permission tests here; retrofitting them is misery.
P2 · 2 wk
Portfolio, tenants & vacancy
Properties, units, leases, the tenant record, CSV import of the current rent roll, and the vacancy dashboard with unit_status_history. Milestone: the portfolio lives in the app and the landlord can see occupancy at a glance.
P3 · 2–3 wk
Caretaker repair app ← ship first to a real user
Mobile logging with camera capture and client-side compression, unit picker, four-state workflow, photo timeline, landlord triage board, offline outbox, push notifications. Put it in the caretaker's hand in week 6 and watch them use it.
P4 · 1–2 wk
Electricity module
Meters, single and batch capture, CSV import, receipt uploads, duplicate-token and rate-anomaly validation, per-unit and per-property history with trends, optional recharge-to-tenant.
P5 · 3 wk
Ledger, invoicing & statements
Ledger primitives, monthly invoice generation, invoice and statement PDFs, emailed delivery with delivery tracking, manual payment recording, late-fee engine, arrears view. Reconcile against the spreadsheet for a full month before switching over.
P6 · 1 wk
Offboarding & deletion
The exit checklist, archive PDF, the atomic offboarding transaction, anonymise and hard-delete modes, restore drill. Deliberately after the ledger exists — you cannot test safe deletion without data worth protecting.
P7 · 2 wk
Expenses & reports
Expense capture with receipts and tax categories, rent roll, arrears, income and expense statements, cash flow, electricity summary, vacancy and turnover report, CSV/PDF export.
P8 · 1–2 wk
Inspections & hardening
Move-in/move-out and routine inspections on mobile (the caretaker's second job), then the permission audit, backup restore drill, accessibility pass and staff training.
Deferred
Tenant portal, online payments, WhatsApp API, bookkeeper role
All four are additive to this schema, not rewrites of it. Revisit when tenants start asking for a login, or when manual reconciliation stops fitting in an afternoon.
Total: about 13–17 weeks — roughly the same as v0.1 despite four new modules, because the tenant portal and the payment gateway are gone. The caretaker has working software in week 6; the landlord's books move across in week 12.

7 · Three questions this revision raises

1
Is electricity recharged to tenants, or is it a landlord cost?
If it is recharged, the purchase must reach the invoice and I need to know whether at cost or with a markup — that changes the invoice template, the ledger and the reports. If it is a landlord cost, the module is much simpler: expenses plus trends.
2
Can a caretaker close a ticket themselves, or only mark it resolved for landlord sign-off?
I have assumed sign-off: the caretaker sets Resolved, the landlord closes it after seeing the photos and the cost. Full authority is one flag on caretaker_assignments if you would rather trust them.
3
How long must a departed tenant's financial record be kept?
South African tax and company law generally expect several years of retained records, so a true hard delete of invoices may conflict with it. Tell me the retention rule you work to and I will make anonymise the default, with hard delete reserved for a genuine erasure request.
